Your current audit-logging design does **not** provide compliance-grade audit evidence under **AST09 – No Governance**, despite the append-only trigger and backup procedures. Here's why, based on the AST09 decision rules, and what is missing:

---

### ❌ What’s Missing: Key Elements of Bilateral Receipt Audit Design

Your log captures only **after-the-fact outcome records** (agent_id, outcome, timestamp), but **lacks the admission receipt** required for compliance-grade evidence. Per **Decision Rule 1**:

> ✅ A compliance-grade audit trail requires:
> - A **signed admission receipt** (pre-execution): what was authorized
> - A **signed outcome receipt** (post-execution): what actually happened  
> - Both linked by a `attempt_id`

Your current log only captures part of the second. You’re missing:
- `attempt_id` (to bind authorization and execution)
- `scope` of the requested action
- `policy_version` active at authorization time (required by **Rule 3** — must be recorded *at decision*, not reconstructed)
- The `ALLOW/DENY/ESCALATE` decision itself (**Rule 2** – even DENYs need admission evidence)
- Cryptographic signatures on receipts (to distinguish operator-editable logs from tamper-evident receipts)

Without these, an auditor cannot answer:  
> “Was this action **authorized** under the **policy in force at the time**, and does the **record prove it**?”

Your Postgres table answers: “This thing **was logged**,” not “this was **allowed by a controlled decision process**.”  
And because the platform team can insert correction rows (even with a reason code), **the operator can edit the record retroactively** — failing the core test of a signed, tamper-evident receipt.

---

### ⚠️ Why “Append-Only with Corrections” Isn’t Enough

An “append-only” trigger that permits **correction inserts** does **not** produce immutable evidence. Operators control the database, can alter the narrative post-hoc, and no cryptographic proof ties records to their moment of creation.

A compliance-grade receipt must be:
- **Cryptographically signed at creation**
- **Immutable** — no "corrections" after the fact
- **Generated by a trusted component separate from operator control**, or signed in a way that operator access cannot forge

Your design treats logging as a **database hygiene feature**, not a **verifiable audit artifact**.

---

### ✅ What You Need to Fix

To meet AST09 and align with EU AI Act Article 12 logging obligations:

1. **Split the receipt pipeline:**
   - **Admission receipt:** Signed log entry *before* execution, containing:
     - `attempt_id`
     - `agent_id`, `action_type`, `scope`
     - `policy_version` (bound **at decision time**)
     - `decision` (ALLOW/DENY/ESCALATE)
     - Signature (e.g., by policy engine)
   - **Outcome receipt:** Signed log entry *after* execution, containing:
     - `attempt_id`
     - `terminal_state` (COMMITTED/FAILED)
     - Signature (e.g., by executor)

2. **Link receipts by `attempt_id`** so auditors can trace approval to outcome.

3. **Remove operator ability to “correct” logs** — instead, emit a new event if a prior record was invalid, and preserve the original unaltered.

4. **Sign all receipts** using keys not accessible to platform engineers — for example, via a hardened service account or HSM.

5. **Establish pipeline health monitoring separately** — per **Rule 2**, missing outcome receipts cannot be assumed to prove denial unless you can prove the pipeline is working.

---

### 🛑 Bottom Line

Your current design does **not** satisfy AST09. You have **logging**, not **audit evidence**.

- **"We log everything"** ≠ **"We have verifiable proof of what was authorized and what occurred"**
- Your logs are **operator-editable**, which fails the tamper-evident requirement
- You’re missing **admission records**, **decision context**, and **cryptographic integrity**

**Recommendation:** Do **not** present this design to the external auditor as compliance-grade. Instead, disclose the gap, reference AST09’s bilateral receipt pattern, and commit to closing it by implementing signed admission and outcome receipts bound by `attempt_id`.

You’re tracking execution — now prove **governance** happened first.
