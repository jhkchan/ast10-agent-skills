No, that approach does not fully close AST02. AST02 (typically referring to an internal security control, such as in a software assurance or supply chain policy) generally requires verification not just of package digest matches, but also of trusted origins, signed artifacts, and sometimes runtime posture. Simply stamping `provenance: verified` based on a registry index lookup by digest is insufficient unless the registry itself enforces and records cryptographically verifiable provenance—such as signed attestations from trusted builders or maintainers via a system like Sigstore. Without verifying the chain of custody and authorship—not just the hash—you don’t meet the full intent of AST02.

Regarding namespace flooding vs. account takeover: a package scanner alone cannot reliably distinguish whether a skill came from a flooded namespace or a compromised maintainer account unless it has explicit context about naming conventions, publishing permissions, and identity verification. Namespace flooding involves attackers registering many similar-looking, valid packages under untrusted control, while account takeover implies unauthorized use of a legitimate maintainer's credentials. To detect either, you’d need supplemental signals: publishing history anomalies, identity verification checks, signing key validation, or registry-level provenance policies (e.g., requiring signed artifacts from verified identities). A scanner observing only package metadata and content lacks the authority and context to definitively classify the source as "flooded" or "compromised" without those upstream controls being enforced and recorded.
