No, this approach does not fully close AST02. AST02 typically refers to risks around supply chain integrity, such as verifying that a package is genuinely from the claimed source and has not been tampered with. While using a digest to verify package content via a registry index improves confidence in integrity, it doesn’t ensure the authenticity of the original source unless the index itself is cryptographically signed and the lookup enforces trust in provenance metadata. Simply matching a digest and stamping `provenance: verified` without validating the trust chain (e.g., who published it, signing keys, transparency log inclusion) leaves room for spoofed or impersonated packages if namespace or account compromises occur.

Regarding the second question: a package scanner alone cannot reliably determine whether a skill originated from a flooded namespace versus a compromised maintainer account unless it has access to detailed provenance metadata—such as publisher identity, signing assertions, publication logs, or certificate transparency data—and the ability to interpret policy against it. Without explicit metadata indicating expected publisher identities or namespace policies (e.g., "only packages signed by X key are valid for namespace Y"), the scanner lacks the context to make that distinction. You’d need to combine the scanner with a policy engine that enforces namespace ownership rules and verifies cryptographic signatures from trusted maintainers.
